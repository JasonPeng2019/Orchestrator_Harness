## Public interface and compatibility boundary

Consumes one frozen reviewable input, gate question, checking-member contracts, prior credit, and isolated allocations. Produces one complete joined result pool. Changing shared input, review class, member proof surface, materiality, check selection, or result contract invalidates the instance; internal scheduling with the same two-member contract does not.

## Rules, process, recipe actions, and allowed variations

P01 through P11 and P13 through P15 apply. Every campaign has one ROOT governing card plus exactly one REVIEWER and one CHECKER member. Findings are open within their bounded surfaces. Both members launch before either is awaited and write disjoint roots; ordinary failure never cancels feasible work.

#### Outputs and results

Two terminal handoffs joined once into RESULT-PRODUCT-CAMPAIGN with findings, unit results, skips, disagreements, and credit map.

#### Concurrency and isolation

ROOT launches review and check before awaiting either. Each has its own revision-pinned worktree, invocation, cache, and result root.

#### Resources and side effects

Read-only product source; test caches and result files stay within assigned roots. No real external resource.

#### Checks and acceptance

INITIAL_IMPLEMENTATION review plus focused compile/unit/package/docs checks. M05 and ROOT decide product acceptance.

#### Failure and exception routes

Ordinary failures join. Failed prerequisite skips only dependent paths. Support fault remains explicit. P15 cannot trigger absent live effects.

#### Prior results and change effects

Cold addendum entry has no review credit for changed gaps; accepted baseline credit remains only where declared inputs and behavior are unchanged. A repaired tip preserves only results whose declared inputs remain unchanged and reruns affected surfaces.

#### Repeat, join, and terminal behavior

Finish all feasible members, join once, and hand to M05. Affected repeat retains roles and never reviews intermediate repair revisions.

#### Cost and critical-path effect

Two worker launches overlap. This costs one extra lane versus serial checking and saves the longer of review/check durations.
